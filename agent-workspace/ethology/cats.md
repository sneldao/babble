# Felid vocal reference

Cats vocalize at humans far more than at each other — adult cats rarely meow
to other cats. Meows are a human-directed channel.

## Classes

- **Solicitation / food-seeking** — short-to-medium meow (0.3–0.9s), rising or
  arching f0 (400–700 Hz), repeated. Higher fundamental + rising contour reads
  more urgent. Often delivered near food locations or at door/meal times.
- **Greeting / trill (chirrup)** — very short (0.1–0.35s), rising-warbled
  contour, medium-high f0, low intensity. Friendly acknowledgment.
- **Protest / complaint** — longer (0.8–2s), falling or falling-flat contour,
  lower f0 with drooping tail of the call. Door closed, carrier, bath.
- **Distress** — long (1–3s), loud, high intensity envelope, unstable/warbled
  f0, wide f0 range. Pain or acute fear — flag honestly, suggest checking on
  the animal.
- **Hiss / defensive** — aperiodic (voiced_ratio very low), high spectral
  centroid, moderate duration. Not a meow at all: a warning.
- **Purr / contentment** — very low f0 (< 150 Hz if resolved), long duration,
  steady rhythmic envelope. Rarely picked up on a laptop mic.

## Posture cues (when a frame is attached)

- Tail upright, tip curled → affiliation. Tail lashing, puffed → arousal/threat.
- Ears forward → engaged; flattened sideways/back → fear or aggression.
- Slow blink, body loaf → relaxed. Crouched, pupils dilated → aroused.
