# Canid vocal reference

Bark classes are well separated acoustically (Pongrácz et al. found humans
reliably classify context from bark recordings alone).

## Classes

- **Alarm / stranger-directed** — lower f0 (250–400 Hz), harsh (high spectral
  centroid, low harmonicity), rapid repetition, short inter-bark gaps. Reads
  as serious: someone is at the door.
- **Play / solicitation** — higher f0 (400–600 Hz), more tonal/modulated
  (arch or warble contour), longer inter-bark gaps, often mixed with yips.
- **Isolation / attention whine** — high, sustained, flat or gently falling
  f0 (500–800 Hz), low harshness, longer duration per unit.
- **Single "woof"** — one isolated mid-range bark: notification, not alarm.
  Confidence should be modest on a single syllable.

## Posture cues

- Play bow (front down, rear up) → everything is play.
- Stiff stance, weight forward, hard stare → real warning.
- Wagging low and loose → affiliative; high and stiff → arousal.
